# Lesson plan with the 5E model

## Purpose
Draft a complete lesson plan structured as Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate. Works best for inquiry-style lessons in science, mathematics and technology.

## Prompt
```text
You are an experienced {{subject}} teacher and curriculum designer.

Write a lesson plan for grade {{grade}} on the topic "{{topic}}".

Context:
- Lesson length: {{duration}} minutes
- Class size: {{class_size}}
- Available materials and technology: {{resources}}
- Prior knowledge students already have: {{prior_knowledge}}

Structure the plan with the 5E model. For each phase give: the teacher's actions,
the students' actions, the time in minutes, and one question the teacher can ask.
The minutes of all phases must add up to exactly {{duration}}.

Also include:
1. Two or three measurable learning objectives that start with an action verb.
2. The most likely misconception students hold about this topic and how to address it.
3. One quick way to check understanding at the end.

Write in {{output_language}}. Use plain language a new teacher can follow.
Do not invent statistics or cite sources.
```

## Variables
| Variable | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `subject` | School subject | Science |
| `grade` | Grade level | 6 |
| `topic` | Lesson topic | States of matter |
| `duration` | Minutes available | 40 |
| `class_size` | Number of students | 28 |
| `resources` | Materials and technology you really have | Ice, water bottles, no projector |
| `prior_knowledge` | What students already know | They know solid and liquid, not gas |
| `output_language` | Language of the answer | English |

## Tips
- Fill in `resources` honestly. Plans that assume a projector are useless in a room without one.
- Ask a follow-up such as "Shorten the Explore phase to 10 minutes and rebalance the rest" instead of rewriting the whole prompt.

## Watch out for
- Time estimates are guesses. Check that the Explore phase is realistic for your class.
- Misconceptions suggested by the model should be checked against your own experience or a reliable source.
